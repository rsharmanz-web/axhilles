Every task on the status board got there by moving through the same five gates — this is that process, made explicit, so the next hundred ideas go through it too instead of getting decided case by case.

It's a double-diamond shape: the first diamond diverges to find the real pain and converges on a specific, well-defined task; the second diverges into a rough build and converges on something proven under real conditions. Live isn't a sixth step so much as what happens once the second diamond closes — the task graduates from project to practice.

The five gates
Diamond 1 · Diverge
01

Discovery

Purpose
Open discovery conversations, not scoped requirements — find where the real, recurring pain actually is.
Exit gate
Is there a real, recurring workflow pain here, and could a tool plausibly help?
Owner
Whoever raises it, sense-checked by a partner
Typical duration
1–2 conversations
Diamond 1 · Converge
02

Exploration

Purpose
Narrow the pain to one specific task, classify its lens (coordination / evidence / judgement), and rate its feasibility.
Exit gate
Lens assigned, feasibility rated, and a one-sentence path to a prototype exists.
Owner
Partner + the IC who does the work
Typical duration
Same session, or a short follow-up
Diamond 2 · Diverge
03

Proof of Concept

Purpose
Build the smallest thing that tests the real risk — on an actual example, not a synthetic one.
Exit gate
Did it save real time or produce a defensible output, on a real file?
Owner
Builder + one IC as first user
Typical duration
Hours to a few days, time-boxed
Diamond 2 · Converge
04

Field Testing

Purpose
Run it inside a live engagement with more than one user, under real file-review scrutiny.
Exit gate
Measured error rate / time saved, reviewed and signed off by a partner — no reportable quality issues.
Owner
Partner sign-off required, especially evidence / judgement lens
Typical duration
One full engagement cycle
Operate
05

Live

Purpose
Rolled into standard practice: documented, owned, and revisited as standards or tools change.
Exit gate (ongoing)
Still fit for purpose at the next scheduled review.
Owner
Whoever owns the practice's tool inventory
Typical duration
Ongoing — reviewed annually
