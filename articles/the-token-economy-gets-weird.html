Brace yourself, the token economy is going to be weird

Most people will end up paying for AI whether they chose to or not, through dozens of small meters across tools they already use.

Rahul Sharma 6 October 2026 2 min read

For the past few months I’ve been working across Claude Cowork, ChatGPT and Cursor, and dabbling with [tool]. I’m in start-up mode, so every subscription has to earn its place. I’ve settled on Cursor Pro for coding and agents and Claude Pro for Cowork and Claude Design, which comes to $80 a month.

That makes me unusual. ChatGPT has 900 million weekly users, but only about 50 million of them pay.1 Microsoft has sold 15 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats, which is around 3% of its 450 million commercial seats.2

So the industry is changing tack. Rather than waiting for people to opt in, it’s building AI into the price. Microsoft added Copilot to its Personal and Family plans and put the US price up by $3 a month, 43% on the Personal plan.3 Meanwhile, the SaaS tools that spent the last few years bolting AI onto their products are starting to meter it. Salesforce charges 10 cents per agent action.4 From December, Atlassian will bill for AI credits and automation steps on top of seats.5

Most people will end up paying for AI whether or not they chose to, through dozens of small meters spread across tools they already use.

The part I’m less worried about is running out. Humans are clever. [Your example: e.g. which jobs you now give the expensive model, which go to a cheaper one, and which you’ve stopped using AI for.] Once you start paying attention, tokens go a surprisingly long way.

Which is where it gets weird. We’re heading for a world where knowledge workers check their token balance the way teenagers in 2005 checked how many texts they had left.
