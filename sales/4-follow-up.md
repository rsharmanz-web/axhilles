# 4. Follow up

What happens on day 2, day 9, and week 6.

Most cold outbound fails here, not on the phone. One call and one email is not a cadence, and
"just checking in" is not a touch. The rules below hold for every sequence in this document:

- **Every touch carries something new.** New information, new angle, new proof. If you have nothing
  new, the touch is not due yet.
- **Every touch proposes a specific next step.** A named time, or a yes/no question they can answer
  in four words.
- **Two voicemails per sequence, maximum.** After that, switch channel.
- **Multi-channel beats repetition.** Call, email and LinkedIn in rotation. Five emails in a row is
  a spam filter's dream.
- **Reference the last touch.** "I left you a message Tuesday" makes it a thread instead of a
  stranger's fourth attempt.
- **Log it, or it did not happen.**
- **Always leave the door open.** How you exit decides whether you are welcome in six months.

## Cadence A — Cold, never connected

Twelve touches across roughly 15 business days. Tier A and B get all of it; tier C skips to nurture.

| Day | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Email | Three lines. Their trigger, one sentence on us, one question. No attachment |
| 2 | Call | Full opener. No voicemail |
| 2 | LinkedIn | Connection request, no note |
| 4 | Call | Different time of day. **Voicemail 1** |
| 5 | Email | Reply on the same thread. One concrete example from their vertical |
| 8 | Call | Third time window. No voicemail |
| 9 | LinkedIn | Message: one line, one link to a relevant article from `/readings/` |
| 11 | Call | **Voicemail 2**, naming the offer and the price |
| 12 | Email | The one-pager that matches your best guess at their problem |
| 15 | Email | **The close-out.** See below |
| 15 | — | Move to nurture or disqualify `no-contact` |

**The close-out email does more work than the other eleven touches combined.**

> Subject: closing the loop
>
> Sarah — I've tried you a few times about the automation role and how your team's actually using AI.
> Wrong time, or wrong person? Either answer is useful and I'll act on it.
>
> If it's just noise, say "not now" and I'll stop. I'll send one thing a month that's genuinely worth
> reading, and leave it at that.
>
> Rahul

Give them a costless way to say no, and a surprising number instead tell you the truth.

### Email rules for this cadence

Three sentences. One question. No attachment on the first two. Subject lines in lower case, under
five words, and looking like something a person typed: `automation lead role`, `one question`,
`the audit file thing`. Signature is a name and a mobile number, not a banner.

## Cadence B — Connected, not now

They talked, they were not hostile, there is no meeting. Do not keep dialling — earn the next
conversation instead.

| When | Touch |
|---|---|
| Same day | Email: thank them, restate their problem in their own words, attach the matching one-pager, no ask |
| Week 2 | Something useful with no ask at all — an article, a relevant tool, a note about a competitor's move |
| Week 6 | Call with a **new reason**: a case study, a cohort starting, a shift in their vertical |
| Quarterly | Nurture list |

The week 6 call is not a follow-up, it is a new first call. You need a genuinely new reason to exist:

> "Sarah, Rahul from Axhilles. When we spoke in August you said the annual accounts process was the
> painful one. I've just finished something almost identical with another practice and I thought of
> your comment. Two minutes?"

**"Call me after busy season"** is its own case: diarise the exact date, and open the callback with
*"you told me to ring back this week"*. That single line converts better than anything else in this
playbook, because it is true and they remember saying it.

## Cadence C — Meeting booked

Protects the 80% held-rate assumption. Without this, expect 60%.

| When | Touch |
|---|---|
| Immediately | Calendar invite, while still on the phone |
| Same day | Confirmation email: the one thing you'll cover, **two questions to think about**, one link. Under 100 words |
| Day before, 4pm | One line: "Still good for 10am tomorrow? Anything you'd like me to look at first?" |
| 1 hour before | Nothing. Over-confirming reads as needy |

**Confirmation email**

> Subject: thursday 10am — two questions
>
> Sarah — confirmed for Thursday 10am, 30 minutes. No deck.
>
> Two things to mull over:
> 1. Which process, if it got 30% faster, would you actually notice?
> 2. Who in the team is already using AI without being asked?
>
> If it's useful, this is the four-week programme most firms start with: axhilles.com/offers/building-a-habit.html
>
> Rahul

**No-show protocol.** Call five minutes in. If no answer, email within the hour: *"Lost you —
diaries happen. Here's the same slot next week, or send me a better one."* Re-offer once. After a
second no-show, drop to nurture; two no-shows is an answer.

## Cadence D — After Discovery

The highest-value cadence in the playbook, and the easiest to fumble by going quiet while you write
a perfect proposal.

| When | Touch |
|---|---|
| Within 24h | **The recap.** What I heard, what I'd recommend, what it costs, what happens next |
| Day 3 | The one asset that answers their specific objection — case study, article, the spine board |
| Day 7 | Call. Not "any thoughts?" but "what did your partners say when you raised it?" |
| Day 12 | Email: name the obstacle you think is real and offer to help with it |
| Day 18 | **The decision-forcing email.** See below |
| Day 21 | Close as won, or `timing-hard` with a re-look date |

**The recap email is the proposal for anything under about $5,000.** One page, their language, no
preamble:

> Subject: what I heard, and what I'd do
>
> Sarah — good conversation. Back to you on what I took from it.
>
> **What I heard.** Annual accounts prep takes your team roughly 12 hours a week of reformatting and
> chasing, and the two people who are good with AI are doing it quietly on their own.
>
> **What I'd do.** Building a habit, four people, four weeks. Two-hour setup, weekly 30-minute
> one-to-ones on live files, showcase in week 4 where they demo what they built. $750 +GST per person,
> so $3,000 — and 20% off if you get to five.
>
> **Why start there rather than bigger.** You don't need a strategy, you need evidence. In four weeks
> you'll have something built and four people who can repeat it, and that tells you whether a wider
> piece is worth doing.
>
> **Next step.** I've held the week of the 14th. Say the word and I'll send the intake form.
>
> Rahul

**The decision-forcing email** at day 18 — direct, unembarrassed, and easy to answer:

> Subject: park it?
>
> Sarah — no movement on this, which usually means one of three things: the timing's wrong, the price
> is wrong, or something else got more urgent. All fine, I'd just rather know which.
>
> Want me to park it until after March?
>
> Rahul

## Cadence E — Won

The sale is the start of the next one. Habit cohorts are built for this: week 4 is a showcase, which
means the client presents our value to their own colleagues.

| When | Touch |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Intake form out, dates locked, and ask *"who else should see the week 4 showcase?"* |
| Week 4 | At the showcase: invite the sponsor, the referring partner, and one leader who has not bought yet |
| Week 4 + 2 days | The "what next" recommendation — usually Alpha Prototype on the best thing built |
| Week 6 | **Ask for the referral**, specifically: "who else in your network is being asked about AI with no good answer?" |
| Quarterly | Value touch. Stay in their world |

Two rules: **ask for the referral at the moment of maximum goodwill**, which is the showcase, not
three months later. And **name the person you want** — "who else in your Baker Tilly group?" beats
"anyone come to mind?"

## Cadence F — Nurture

Where tier C and every polite no lives. Cheap to run, and it is where next year's pipeline is.

**Monthly, one email to the whole list.** One genuinely good article from `/readings/`, two lines on
why it matters, no pitch. Aim for the thing someone forwards to a colleague.

**Quarterly, a personal touch to anyone who ever had a real conversation.** One line referencing
what they told you last time. No ask.

**Event-driven, immediately.** These beat any scheduled touch, so watch for them:

- They change role → congratulations, and a new-role conversation is the easiest one there is
- They post about AI → comment, then call two days later
- They hire for automation → the trigger is back and the score jumps
- Their competitor announces something → forward it with one line
- They open the same email three times → Chax and the Vercel analytics will tell you; call them

**Exit rules.** Unsubscribe is honoured immediately and permanently. Two years of no engagement means
delete, not archive.

## Scheduling the work

Cadences only work if the due dates surface somewhere you look. In
[`pipeline-tracker.csv`](pipeline-tracker.csv), `next_action` and `next_action_date` are the only two
columns that must never be blank on a live lead, and `cadence` records which sequence a lead is on.
Which cadence a lead earns is set by its tier — see [`3-rank.md`](3-rank.md).

**Wednesday's follow-up block, in order:**

1. Anything overdue, oldest first
2. Cadence D touches — post-Discovery leads are the closest to revenue
3. Cadence C confirmations for the coming week
4. Cadence A touches due today
5. Whatever event-driven triggers turned up

If the block runs out of time, the bottom of that list is what slips. Never the top.
